# LP-0029: Lending & Borrowing App [DRAFT]

**`Status: Draft`**
**`Logos Circle: N/A`**

## Overview

This prize is for a Logos Basecamp app that lets people lend, borrow, and deposit into curated vaults on the Logos Execution Zone (LEZ), and for getting that app used on Logos testnet 0.4.

The on-chain side is funded separately. [RFP-008 (Lending & Borrowing Protocol)](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/RFPs/RFP-008-lending-borrowing-protocol.md) delivers an isolated-market lending program in the style of Morpho Blue, with a core module, a CLI, a protocol GUI, and a reference liquidator and risk monitor. [RFP-012 (Curated Lending Vaults)](https://github.com/logos-co/rfp/blob/master/RFPs/RFP-012-curated-lending-vaults.md) adds vaults that allocate one deposit across several of those markets. This prize covers what those RFPs leave out: an app a lender or borrower without DeFi experience can use from a private account, and live markets and vaults on testnet 0.4 that people actually supply to and borrow from.

## Motivation

In the Morpho model, anyone can create a market by picking a loan token, a collateral token, an oracle, an interest rate model, and a liquidation LTV (LLTV). The protocol does not judge whether a market is safe: lenders are expected to check the oracle and the LLTV before they supply. A protocol GUI shows these parameters; most users need them explained, and many will prefer a vault where a curator has done that work. The app is where that explanation happens.

Protocol state on LEZ is public. Privacy comes from the client: the user acts from a private account, the client deshields to a fresh public account, runs the operation, and reshields the result. Whether a user gets privacy in practice depends on how well the app handles that path, including the positions that stay open for weeks.

A lending program with no markets, no supply, and no borrowers is not useful to anyone. Getting from deployed programs to markets people use takes product work and community work that an RFP for protocol software does not cover. Several teams can build on the same deployed programs and make different product choices, which suits a competitive prize judged on adoption.

## Success Criteria

### Functionality

- [ ] **Deployed programs, unmodified.** The app works against the RFP-008 lending program and the RFP-012 vault program as deployed on testnet 0.4. Program IDs will be listed in [Resources](#resources) when this prize opens. Changes to either program, or a separate deployment of a modified copy, are out of scope.
- [ ] **Lend**: browse markets, supply the loan token to a market, and withdraw it.
- [ ] **Borrow**: deposit collateral, borrow, repay in part or in full, and withdraw collateral.
- [ ] **Vaults**: deposit into a vault and redeem vault shares. For each vault, show the markets it allocates to, its caps, its curator, its performance fee, and its timelock.
- [ ] **Market details**: for each market, show the loan and collateral tokens, the oracle program ID and its current price, the LLTV, utilisation, supply APY, borrow APR, and available liquidity.
- [ ] **Portfolio**: one view of the user's positions across all markets and vaults, for each of their accounts, public and private: supplied, borrowed, collateral, vault shares, current value, and accrued interest.
- [ ] **Liquidation risk**: for each borrow position, show the current LTV, the market's LLTV, and the collateral price at which the position becomes liquidatable. Before a borrow or a collateral withdrawal, show the LTV after the operation. While the app is running, warn the user when a position approaches its LLTV. A position that has been liquidated shows what was repaid and what collateral was seized.
- [ ] **Private accounts**: every operation can be run from a private account using the deshield, interact, reshield pattern set out in RFP-008 Privacy Architecture: a fresh public account per operation, gas funded within the deshield, and a reshield step that cannot be skipped. Before the user confirms, the app shows what will be public on-chain and what stays private.
- [ ] **Testnet 0.4**: the app operates end to end against the official Logos LEZ zone on testnet 0.4.

### Usability

- [ ] A Logos Basecamp app (ui module) with its business logic in one or more core modules, so other Logos apps can read positions and run operations without this app's UI. The RFP-008 and RFP-012 core modules may be reused.
- [ ] Logos Basecamp modules are published to a module catalog: a fork of [`logos-modules-release-base`](https://github.com/logos-co/logos-modules-release-base), publishing releases via [`logos-modules-release-action`](https://github.com/logos-co/logos-modules-release-action). The catalog's `logos-repo.json` URL must be included in the submission so evaluators can install the app through the package-manager UI / `lgpd`.
- [ ] **UX quality.** The app must be usable by someone who has not used a lending protocol before: clear navigation between markets, vaults, and portfolio, plain explanations of LLTV, utilisation, and liquidation, and clear pending and error states. This is judged on overall impression rather than a checklist: evaluators will use the app as an ordinary user would.
- [ ] Every confirmation shows the amount, the market or vault, the estimated fee, and, for borrow positions, the resulting LTV.
- [ ] Operations that would fail (insufficient market liquidity, LLTV exceeded, oracle unavailable, insufficient balance for amount plus fees) are reported with a clear message before anything is submitted.

### Reliability

- [ ] **Interrupted private operations.** If a step of the deshield, interact, reshield sequence fails or the app is closed partway, the app detects funds left in the intermediate public account after restart and lets the user finish the operation or return the funds to their private account.
- [ ] If a market's oracle is unavailable, the app says so and still allows the operations that do not need a price (supply, withdraw supplied tokens, repay).
- [ ] App state (accounts, open positions, pending operations) survives restarts and network drops.
- [ ] **No mandatory centralised services.** Beyond the user's configured sequencer, LEZ node, and Logos protocols, the app must not depend on any third-party web service to function. If one is used (for example a price API or a hosted indexer), it is disclosed to the user, can be disabled, and the app can still show the user's own positions and run every operation with it disabled. Any analytics must be strictly opt-in. Please note that there may be other data protection obligations applicable regarding any collection of analytics which the participant should determine and review for themselves.

### Performance

- [ ] Document the time from confirmation to completed state for each operation, on both the public and the private path, including proof generation, on the hardware used for the demo.

### Supportability

- [ ] Deployed and tested against Logos testnet 0.4.
- [ ] Logos Basecamp modules are built with [`logos-module-builder`](https://github.com/logos-co/logos-module-builder) (`mkLogosModule`). Platform support (recommended): all platforms Logos Basecamp supports. macOS support is checked through the Adoption criteria below.
- [ ] End-to-end integration tests run against a LEZ sequencer in standalone mode with the RFP-008 and RFP-012 programs deployed, and are included in CI. CI is green on the default branch.
- [ ] A reproducible end-to-end demo script works against a real local sequencer with `RISC0_DEV_MODE=0`.
- [ ] A README documents setup, the program IDs used, the markets and vaults set up by the submission, and step-by-step usage of the app.

### Adoption

> These metrics measure traction attributable to a given submission for this prize.
>
> **All of the following are required in addition to the FURPS criteria above**, except where marked *(Discretionary)*. We publish the dimensions we look at, but hitting a raw number is not by itself sufficient (see [Terms & Conditions](../TERMS.md) for the evaluation policy that applies to all prizes). Evaluators will inspect commit histories, on-chain activity, and the posting history of vouching accounts. Volume that is clearly manufactured (fresh accounts, coordinated bursts, purchased engagement) does not count, regardless of the headline number.

Lending instructions do not carry a submission identifier, and positions opened through the private path come from fresh accounts by design. On-chain activity is therefore attributed through the markets created and the vaults curated by the submission: the submission publishes the creator account for its markets and the curator account for its vaults, and counts below refer to activity in those markets and vaults.

- [ ] **30 positions** (supply, borrow, or vault deposit) opened by accounts independent of the submitting team, of which at least **10 are borrow positions** and at least **10 are vault deposits**.
- [ ] **10 borrow positions repaid in full** by accounts independent of the submitting team.
- [ ] **Sustained activity**: the 30 positions are spread over at least **2 months**, with at least **10 opened in each of those months**. A single burst does not qualify, however large. Positions opened through the private path cannot be tied to an identity; evaluators weigh them on their pattern over time (sizes, durations, repayment) rather than on account history. Accounts funded from a common source or created in a burst around the submission date do not count.
- [ ] **5 reproducible issues** opened by people outside the submitting team and resolved with a linked fix, with a median first response under 3 days over the observation window.
- [ ] Adoption evidence (issues, testimonials) includes **macOS** users as well as Linux, so that macOS support is shown by use.
- *(Discretionary)* Testimonials from people who used the app, each describing what they did with it. Evaluators will contact a random sample; unanswered or generic replies count against the submission.
- *(Discretionary)* Liquidations in the submission's markets executed by liquidators independent of the submitting team.
- *(Discretionary)* Tutorials, walkthroughs, or talks by people outside the submitting team that show working use of the app.

**Not counted.** Stars, forks without divergent commits, follower counts, likes and reposts, package download counts, and activity by the submitting team, Logos, or Logos CCs.

> **Getting tokens to users.** Users need the loan and collateral tokens of a market before they can supply or borrow. How they obtain them is not a criterion of this prize and is left to the submission.

## Additional Design Choices Left to the Submitter

The following are deliberately not set out in the Success Criteria, as they are additional design choices which the submitter should decide upon. A submission must pick a position on each, document it, and justify it: the reasoning is part of what is evaluated.

- **Keeping control of a private position.** RFP-008 requires a fresh public account for each operation from a private account, but a borrow or vault position lives in one account for as long as it is open, and later operations (repay, add collateral, redeem) must act on it. State how the app keeps the user in control of that position (for example by keeping the position account's key with the user's private keys, or through RFP-008's delegated position management) and what an observer can link between the operations on one position.
- **Which markets to show.** Market creation is permissionless, so some markets will have poor oracles or unsafe parameters. The app may list every market, a curated list, or a list the user controls, and may show oracle source verification from a program registry meeting or similar to the specifications of [LP-0023](LP-0023.md). State what the default is and how the user is warned about a market they have not seen before.
- **Finding markets and positions.** RFP-008 does not include an indexer. State how the app discovers markets, vaults, and the user's positions, and what runs on the user's machine to do so.
- **Key custody.** The app may sign through a Logos wallet module (for example one meeting the specifications of [LP-0021](LP-0021.md)) or manage keys itself. If it manages keys, state how they are backed up.

## Scope

### In Scope

- The Basecamp app and its core module(s): markets, vaults, portfolio, liquidation risk, and the private interaction path.
- Creating markets and running curated vaults on testnet 0.4 using the RFP-008 and RFP-012 programs.
- Tests, CI, catalog publication, documentation, and the demo.

### Out of Scope

- Changes to the RFP-008 lending program, the RFP-012 vault program, or the interest rate model.
- Building oracle programs. Markets use oracles available on LEZ.
- Liquidator and risk-monitor software. RFP-008 delivers reference implementations; a submission may use them.
- Leverage looping and one-click multiply products, which both RFPs also exclude.
- Hosting an indexer or any other service for users.
- Mainnet deployment.

## Evaluation Process

Submissions are evaluated first-come-first-served against the success criteria. The first submission that meets all criteria wins.

Because the adoption criteria require sustained activity over at least two months, a submission cannot qualify until that window has elapsed. Builders are encouraged to release early and start accruing adoption while continuing to develop, rather than treating the build and the adoption push as sequential.

Evaluators will independently clone the repository and run the demo script from a clean environment; the script must succeed without modification. Evaluators may also ask technical follow-up questions to verify authorship and understanding of the implementation.

The following policies apply to all prizes (see [evaluation policies](../README.md#evaluation-policies)):

- **Submissions:** each builder (or team) is allowed a maximum of **3 submissions** per prize, with at most **one submission/review per week**.
- **Feedback:** initial evaluation feedback is limited to a pass/fail indication against the success criteria.
