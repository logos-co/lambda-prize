# LP-0027: Atomic-Swap Maker Operator Kit — three independent testnet makers [DRAFT]

**`Status: Draft`**
**`Logos Circle: N/A`**

## Overview

This prize is for an **operator kit** that lets someone who is not the software's author stand up and keep running a BTC↔LEZ atomic-swap **Maker** on the official Logos LEZ testnet and Bitcoin testnet4, and for the outcome that matters: **three independent operators actually run makers with it for 30 consecutive days**.

The swap software itself exists. RFP-003 delivers the Maker Node, its owner JSON-RPC API, the Maker and Taker CLIs, the Basecamp desks and a public-testnet run guide. What does not exist is everything an operator needs around that: a pinned, reproducible bring-up of the whole stack on a host they control; a policy layer that keeps an offer live and sensibly priced without a human watching; probes and alerts that say whether the maker is actually serving; a backup and restore path for the keys and state that cannot be lost; and a runbook written for an operator rather than a developer. This prize funds that layer and pays for proof that strangers can use it.

## Motivation

A swap protocol with one maker is a demo. The supply side of LEZ↔Bitcoin liquidity only becomes decentralised when unaffiliated people run makers on hosts they own, with keys they hold, and keep them up through testnet resets, chain stalls and their own mistakes. Today the reference implementation targets one operator, one host, arm64, and a developer who can read the Compose files. The public-testnet guide requires the operator to run their own Bitcoin Core, a Logos Blockchain node, an LEZ indexer and a proxy, and to deploy the escrow program themselves. That is a high wall for an operator, and the raw daemon publishes a fixed-ratio offer with no repricing, no inventory limits and no scheduler.

A competitive prize fits this better than an RFP because the deliverable is judged by whether other people adopt it, not by whether a contractor shipped a spec. The kit author has to make onboarding easy enough that three strangers finish it; the operators are recruited and rewarded on the same terms whoever builds the kit.

**Evaluation support.** The Logos Ecosystem Development team runs the external offer probe described below, keeps the operator register, and will broker introductions to candidate operators in the Logos community. Logos does not build, host, operate or take swaps with any maker; every check is made from public chain data, from offers read on Logos Delivery, and from interviews.

## Success Criteria

### Functionality

- [ ] **Pinned upstream, no fork of the core.** The kit runs an **unmodified** RFP-003 Maker release, identified by tag, commit and image or binary digest, and records that pin in every deployment. Patches to the Node, actors, CLIs or desks are out of scope; the kit may only configure, wrap and drive them through the documented owner API.
- [ ] **One-command bring-up on the operator's own host** of everything a Maker needs against the **official LEZ testnet** (`https://testnet.lez.logos.co`) and **Bitcoin testnet4**: the Maker Node and its actors, the Bitcoin backend (own Bitcoin Core, or the documented keyless public-provider route where the release supports it), the Logos Blockchain node or a documented remote alternative, the LEZ indexer and sequencer proxy, and the escrow program deployment or attachment to the published one. The operator supplies host, keys, inventory and configuration; the kit supplies the rest.
- [ ] **Key and identity ownership stays with the operator.** The kit generates or imports the Delivery identity, LEZ signer, Bitcoin wallet and claim keys on the operator's host. Nothing leaves that host except signed transactions and published offers. Owner-socket access is never exposed over the network.
- [ ] **Policy layer over the owner API**: publish an offer from a configured reference price with a configurable spread and lot bounds; refresh or withdraw and republish on price change, TTL expiry or inventory change; cap exposure per pair and in total; pause publishing when `maker_health` reports degraded, when a wallet is below its floor, or when the operator says so. The policy is driven by the release's price-source mechanism or a documented external feed adapter; it does not modify the Node.
- [ ] **Operator observability**: a local health and status view (Node health, delivery and chat state, live offers, active swaps, wallet balances, last failure), alerting to at least one operator-chosen channel (e-mail, webhook or messaging), and a one-command **evidence export** for any swap using the release's export tooling.
- [ ] **Backup and restore**: a documented, scripted backup of the critical state classes named by the release (Maker database, Delivery signing identity, wallet identities, escrow deployment manifest) and a **restore drill** that brings a maker back on a fresh host with its open swaps recovering, without loss of funds.
- [ ] **Upgrade path**: move a running maker to a newer pinned release with open swaps handled per the release's documented recovery and repin behaviour.

### Usability

- [ ] An **operator runbook** written for a systems operator, not a developer: prerequisites, sizing, funding from the public faucets, bring-up, daily checks, what each alert means and what to do, backup, restore, upgrade, and how to stop cleanly.
- [ ] A first-time operator following only the runbook reaches a live, probe-visible offer in under **one working day** on a clean arm64 Linux host, excluding chain sync time.
- [ ] Configuration lives in one documented file or directory; secrets are separated from configuration.

### Reliability

- [ ] The maker survives a host reboot, a Node restart, a Bitcoin backend outage and a Logos Delivery or Chat outage and resumes serving without operator action, with the outage visible in the status view and alerts.
- [ ] A testnet reset or chain stall is detected and reported rather than silently producing failed swaps; the runbook says what the operator does next.
- [ ] No mandatory centralised service beyond the operator's configured Bitcoin backend, Logos Blockchain node, LEZ sequencer and indexer, and Logos protocols. Any optional third-party service (a price feed, a hosted alert relay) is disclosed, can be disabled, and the maker keeps serving with it disabled.

### Performance

- [ ] Document the offer-refresh latency (price change to new offer visible on Delivery) and the resource footprint of the full stack on the reference host, including disk for the Bitcoin backend.

### Supportability

- [ ] Runs on **Linux arm64**; Linux x86_64 support is reflected favourably where the pinned release allows it.
- [ ] CI on the default branch runs the bring-up, a policy dry-run and the backup/restore drill against the pinned release, and is green.
- [ ] Every friction found by an operator during the observation window is filed as an issue on the kit repository, or on the upstream repository when it belongs there, and either fixed in a tagged kit release or closed with a documented, evaluator-accepted disposition.

### Adoption

> These metrics measure traction attributable to a given submission for this prize.
>
> **All of the following are required in addition to the FURPS criteria above.** We publish the dimensions we look at, but hitting a raw number is not by itself sufficient (see [Terms & Conditions](../TERMS.md) for the evaluation policy that applies to all prizes). Evaluators will inspect hosts, keys, accounts and posting histories. Volume that is clearly manufactured does not count, regardless of the headline number.

- [ ] **Three independent operators** each run a maker with the kit for **30 consecutive days** on the official LEZ testnet and Bitcoin testnet4. The window for an operator starts only after the evaluator has recorded that operator's identity, maker Delivery identity, host and release pin. Deployments that started before registration, and any deployment by the submitting team, by Logos, or by a Logos CC (including the RFP-003 contractor), do not count.
- [ ] **Independence test**, applied to each operator: a different person or organisation from the submitter and from each other; their own host and infrastructure billing account; their own keys, inventory, configuration and backups; their own upgrade and incident decisions; and no submitter access to the host after onboarding. One party running several nominal makers counts once.
- [ ] **Offer availability of at least 90 %** over each operator's window, measured by an **external probe run by Logos** that reads the operator's offers from Logos Delivery. Declared platform-wide outages (testnet reset, sequencer down) pause the clock for everyone; operator-side outages do not.
- [ ] **At least 5 settled swaps per operator** within the window, each with both-chain evidence and each with a taker independent of the operator, the submitter, Logos and every Logos CC. Swaps between an operator and itself, the submitter, or another registered operator do not count. Evaluators will inspect taker accounts and funding flows and may contact takers directly.
- [ ] **One observed recovery drill per operator**: a restart, a refund path, or a dependency outage, recovered without loss of funds and with evidence exported.
- [ ] **Zero unrecovered incidents** attributable to the kit, its defaults or its runbook across all three operators. An incident attributable to the upstream release is recorded against the upstream issue and does not fail the prize if the kit reported it and the operator recovered.
- [ ] **Operator friction closed**: at least **3 issues opened by operators** on the kit repository, each resolved with a linked fix in a tagged release or an evaluator-accepted disposition, with median first response under 3 days.
- [ ] (Discretionary) Written feedback from each operator on setup and operation, describing what they did and what broke. Evaluators will contact operators directly; generic or unanswered replies count against the submission.

**Not counted.** Stars, forks without divergent commits, follower counts, impressions, likes and reposts, waitlist or newsletter signups, package download counts, and site visits are not adoption evidence and are not counted in any position, required or discretionary. Neither are makers run by the submitter, Logos, or a Logos CC, however many.

## Scope

### In Scope

- Packaging, bring-up, configuration and upgrade of an unmodified RFP-003 Maker release on an operator-owned host.
- The policy and scheduling layer over the documented owner API: pricing from a reference, refresh, exposure caps, pause conditions.
- Health, status, alerting and evidence export for operators.
- Backup and restore of the critical state classes, with a drill.
- The operator runbook and onboarding material.
- Recruiting, onboarding and supporting the three operators through their windows.

### Out of Scope

- The Maker or Taker Node, actors, SDKs, CLIs, price-source trait and its reference implementations, Basecamp desks, systemd unit and node-setup documentation. These are delivered under RFP-003 and must be consumed, not rebuilt. A submission that forks or patches them is not eligible.
- Market-making strategy beyond a configurable spread and caps: hedging, rebalancing across venues, simulation, arbitrage. Those are separate applications.
- Mainnet operation, real-value liquidity, spreads, volume and taker counts. Those belong to the mainnet liquidity prize (see below).
- Any pair other than BTC↔LEZ until the corresponding RFP-003 milestone is delivered and this prize is revised.
- Security review of the swap protocol or of the upstream release.

## Eligibility

Open to any individual or team. Submissions must be original work. Teams must hold the rights to all submitted code and agree to license it under both MIT and Apache-2.0. Logos CCs, including the RFP-003 contractor and its sub-contractors, may not submit and are not counted as operators.

## Submission Requirements

- Public repository with the kit, policy layer, probes, backup and restore scripts, runbook and CI, dual-licensed under MIT and Apache-2.0.
- The exact upstream release pin (tag, commit, image or binary digests) the submission was qualified against.
- For each of the three operators: the registration record, the probe availability report produced by Logos, both-chain evidence for the qualifying swaps with taker identities as recorded at swap time, the recovery-drill evidence export, and the operator's feedback.
- The issue list from the observation windows with each item's resolution.
- A narrated video walkthrough of the bring-up, one offer refresh under a price change, one alert firing, and the restore drill, with terminal output visible.
- FURPS self-assessment as part of the solution (see [solution template](../solutions/LP-0000.md)).

## Evaluation Process

Submissions are evaluated first-come-first-served against the success criteria. Because the adoption criteria require three 30-day operator windows, a submission cannot qualify before those windows have elapsed. Logos runs the external offer probe for registered operators of any submission that has passed the functionality gate; operators register with Logos, not with the submitter. Logos does not take swaps.

Evaluators will independently clone the repository and run the bring-up and drill from a clean environment; the scripts must succeed without modification. Evaluators may ask technical follow-up questions to verify authorship and understanding, and will contact operators and takers directly.

The following policies apply to all prizes (see [evaluation policies](../README.md#evaluation-policies)):

- **Submissions:** each builder (or team) is allowed a maximum of **3 submissions** per prize, with at most **one submission/review per week**.
- **Feedback:** initial evaluation feedback is limited to a pass/fail indication against the success criteria.

**Opening condition.** This prize opens only when Logos has pinned an RFP-003 Maker release that has passed its BTC milestone review and whose public-testnet run guide is current, and the official LEZ testnet is stable enough to hold a 30-day window. Until then it stays in Draft.
